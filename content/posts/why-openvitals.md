15 years ago I installed my first distro of ubuntu, making me fall in love with linux and this idea of open source and community driven projects, slowly but surely I started my deep dive in android modding communities and built the grounds for what was going to be my professional career. 

I always saw technology as a driver of human improvement, something that in kind hands can bring so much good to the world, and even we see that it can also be used for terrible purposes as of this day I have not stopped believing in it.

When I started living in Estonia I got in contact to what I thought it was a digital society utopia, with the palm of your hand you could access all your information and sign any kind of documents, even in your cabin in the middle of the woods.

Even if after 5 years of living there I have a more measured view of Estonia as a whole, I can still say that what they have achieved is unique. Especially regarding health, I was able to access every data of it without the need to store papers, call doctors, track down studies, request consultations. Everything was always at my disposition letting me take the best decisions I could.

At the same time while I was living in Estonia I got a Fitbit device that was quite handy for my trainings and everyday life, and cut a bit of the sedentarism that is so common in my profession, however the more the time passed the more weary i became of giving away so much of my personal data. A bit down the road I decided to move to a Garmin device with Gadgetbridge, a FOSS app to manage devices while my partner took my Fitbit. 

Fast forward this year with the final nail in the coffin of Fitbit and conversion into Google Health, I was surprised with the low effort and bad ux experience, plus serving all your data to Google it was a total [fallout](https://gizmodo.com/google-is-in-full-on-damage-control-over-its-new-health-app-2000764027) plus the insertion of [LLM's](https://www.technology.org/2026/01/13/google-scrubs-faulty-ai-generated-health-advice-that-could-mislead-sick-patients/) output in an uncontrolled and unsafe way. 

This pushed me to start OpenVitals, a health dashboard where you can see all your fitness, wellness and health data with privacy at its heart. The idea is quite simple behind is HealthConnect a private and secure database for all your records, and OpenVitals officiate as a dashboard, plus the ability of manually add entries or record your workouts. 

With this as an endgame we can interconect the whole FOSS ecosystem (Also the non-FOSS one) in terms of fitness, wellness and health 

![OpenTracks, openScale (through openScale-sync), OpenVitals, Gadgetbridge, Hydrotracker and DailySitting all write to Health Connect; the OpenVitals Wear OS app feeds OpenVitals and Gadgetbridge, and OpenVitals also reads back from Health Connect](/images/posts/openvitals-ecosystem.png)

What I valued the most from Estonia was having all my health data at hand to take the best decisions I could, and that is what I want OpenVitals to bring to anyone with a phone, without having to give that data to Google or any other company.

There is still a long way to go, and I would love you to be part of it. You can get OpenVitals on [F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/en/packages/tech.mmarca.openvitals/) or [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=tech.mmarca.openvitals). If something breaks, [open an issue](https://github.com/OpenVitals-MTU/android-app/issues). The code is on [GitHub](https://github.com/OpenVitals-MTU/android-app) and every contribution is welcome, and if you want to support the project you can do it on [Liberapay](https://liberapay.com/manuel.mmarca.tech/donate).

The core idea that 15 years ago Ubuntu showed me what a community can build, I hope OpenVitals can do the same for wellness and health.
